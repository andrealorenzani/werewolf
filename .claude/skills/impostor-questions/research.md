# Research notes (October 2026)

Reusable findings; do not repeat the research, extend it only if something new is needed.

## Existing "question imposter" games
- A web imposter game ("Question Edition") gives most players one question and the Imposter a *different but related*
  one, e.g. "ideal weekend" for most and "memorable vacation" for the Imposter. Its note: an answer like "at the
  beach" can fit both, so a confident answer is not automatically safe. (impostergames.io/question/question-game.html)
- Guides for the format warn against questions that are too specific: they hand the Imposter an easy angle (the
  example of a question that gives too much away: "Is it hot?"). The best pairs reveal little to someone holding the
  other question. Our samples go beyond this: overlapping *shape* of the answer, contrasting *content*.
- Scoring in the wild: points for catching the odd one; the odd one scores by surviving the vote or guessing the real
  question. (Wikipedia "Odd One Out", playantics.com, oneimposter.com, oddone.app)
- No public lists of good pairs exist; the generic examples found were bland (weekend vs vacation). The
  maintainer's 21 samples are far funnier and are the only authority.

## Comedy design (Jackbox, Quiplash prompt writers)
Jackbox's own description of how funny prompts are written (jackboxgames.com, "The Science of Creating a Quiplash
Prompt"):
1. Start from a funny word or ridiculous premise ("what the boogeyman is afraid of").
2. Turn an embarrassing personal experience into a scenario ("the worst song when stripping for a lover").
3. Write for the specific group/event to create inside jokes ("best thing to scream out of a moving party bus").
4. Serious topics treated absurdly give clever answers.
5. Bathroom humour works.
6. Ask things you genuinely want answered ("what are mannequins always thinking?").
Applied here: 1, 2, 5 and 6 map directly onto the samples (baby thoot; shit yourself; browser history; intrusive thoughts).

## Confession-game formats (Never Have I Ever, truth-style lists)
The most reliable laughter comes from confessions about: getting arrested, texting an ex drunk, being fired, bodily
accidents, lies told, embarrassing searches. If only one person owns up, the others ask follow-ups. We use the same
topics, but as a *question* with a quick answer so that the Impostor can fake it.

## Mechanism catalogue (use to vary the pool)
- **Flip**: same sentence, opposite word (turn off / turn on; best / worst).
- **Swap the subject**: same frame, different object ("worst name for a strip club" / "for a kindergarten").
- **Swap the context**: same act, other place ("worst thing to say in sex" / "in a job interview").
- **Number vs number**: embarrassing count vs harmless count ("exes saved in your phone" / "unread emails").
- **Duration vs duration**: longest relationship / longest hangover.
- **Player vs player**: both ask for a player's name, with opposite flattery or danger.
- **Confession vs trivia**: the group confesses, the Impostor answers a fact (retirement money / average wage).
- **Dark vs light**: death, disaster, crime, in one question; the other is silly.
- **Phrase vs phrase**: titles, headlines, bios, quotes, stage names, last words.

## Content limits we keep
Crude and adult is fine (the 21 samples are); no slurs, no hatred towards groups, no minors in a sexual or violent
context, no real private people, no politics or religion, no graphic sexual detail.

## Topic spread (second batch, pairs 95-304)
The first 73 generated pairs leaned on sex, toilet and embarrassment. The 210 that followed deliberately cover: work,
food, travel, school and childhood, tech and the internet, animals and nature, hypotheticals and superpowers, dating
and weddings, money and shopping, music and film, home and daily habits, health and the body, friends and "the
players", crime and mischief, and big/philosophical questions. When extending again, check the topic spread first and
pick the least-covered themes; keep any single theme under ~10% of the pool. The spicy/embarrassing core stays, but
the pool must not feel like one joke.
